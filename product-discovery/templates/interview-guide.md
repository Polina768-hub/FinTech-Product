# User Interview Guide

## Research Topic

**Understanding how young professionals manage their personal finances and what challenges they experience.**

---

# 1. Research Goal

The goal of this research is to understand:

* how young professionals currently manage their personal finances;
* when and why they check their financial situation;
* what difficulties they experience;
* which tools and alternatives they currently use;
* which problems are frequent and important enough to potentially solve.

This research is **not intended to validate a specific product idea**.

At this stage, we are exploring the problem space.

---

# 2. Research Questions

The research should help answer the following questions:

1. How do young professionals currently manage their finances?
2. What situations trigger them to check their financial situation?
3. What are they trying to understand or accomplish?
4. What is difficult or frustrating about the current process?
5. Which tools do they currently use?
6. What alternatives have they tried?
7. How frequently do these problems occur?
8. How important is solving these problems?

---

# 3. Target Participants

## Primary Target User

Young professionals who:

* are approximately 20–35 years old;
* have their own regular or semi-regular income;
* independently manage their personal expenses;
* use at least one banking or financial application;
* make regular financial decisions.

Potential participants may include:

* students with regular income;
* recent graduates;
* early-career professionals;
* freelancers;
* people working their first full-time jobs.

---

# 4. Interview Format

**Format:** Semi-structured interview

**Duration:** 30–45 minutes

**Number of participants:** 5–10

The purpose of a semi-structured interview is to follow a consistent research framework while allowing participants to describe their own experiences.

---

# 5. Introduction Script

> Hi! Thank you for agreeing to talk with me.
>
> I am researching how young professionals currently manage their personal finances and what challenges they experience.
>
> This is not a test, and there are no right or wrong answers. I am interested in your real experiences and behavior.
>
> I am not trying to sell you anything. At this stage, I am simply trying to understand the problem better.
>
> The interview will take approximately 30–45 minutes.
>
> Is it okay if I take notes during our conversation?

---

# 6. Interview Questions

## Section 1 — Context

The goal is to understand the participant's general financial situation without asking for sensitive information.

### Questions

1. Can you tell me a little about how you currently manage your personal finances?

2. How often do you usually check your financial situation?

3. What tools do you currently use to manage or understand your finances?

4. Do you use your banking application only to check your balance, or do you use other features as well?

### Follow-up questions

* Can you tell me more about that?
* What does that usually look like?
* How does that process normally start?

---

# 7. Recent Behavior

The goal is to understand what the participant actually did in the past.

### Questions

1. Tell me about the last time you checked your finances.

2. What caused you to check them?

3. What were you trying to understand?

4. What did you do step by step?

5. Which tool did you use?

6. What happened after that?

### Important

Do not ask:

> Would you use a financial management tool?

Instead ask:

> Tell me what you did the last time you experienced this situation.

Past behavior is usually more useful than hypothetical future behavior.

---

# 8. Pain Points

The goal is to identify difficulties and frustrations.

### Questions

1. What is the most difficult part of managing your finances?

2. Is there anything that takes more time than you would like?

3. Have you ever been surprised by your financial situation?

4. Tell me about the last time that happened.

5. What was difficult to understand?

6. Is there information that you wish was easier to access or understand?

### Follow-up

Ask:

> Why was that difficult?

And then ask again if necessary:

> And why does that matter to you?

The goal is to move from a surface-level answer to the underlying problem.

---

# 9. Spending Behavior

### Questions

1. How do you usually understand where your money goes?

2. How easy or difficult is it for you to notice changes in your spending?

3. Have you ever realized that you spent more than expected?

4. Can you tell me about the most recent time?

5. How did you try to understand what caused the change?

6. Were you able to find an answer?

---

# 10. Current Alternatives

The goal is to understand how users currently solve the problem.

### Questions

1. What do you currently do when you want to better understand your finances?

2. Have you ever tried another tool or method?

3. What did you try?

4. Why did you start using it?

5. Why did you continue or stop using it?

6. What works well in your current approach?

7. What does not work well?

Possible alternatives may include:

* banking applications;
* spreadsheets;
* notes;
* budgeting applications;
* manual calculations;
* advice from friends or family;
* doing nothing.

---

# 11. Frequency and Importance

The goal is to understand whether the identified problem is significant.

### Questions

1. How often does this situation happen?

2. When it happens, how important is it for you to solve it?

3. What happens if you do nothing?

4. Have you ever tried to change your behavior because of this problem?

5. Would solving this problem make a meaningful difference to you? Why?

---

# 12. Closing Questions

1. Is there anything else about managing your finances that you find difficult or frustrating?

2. If you could improve one part of your current financial management process, what would it be?

3. Is there anything important that I did not ask about?

---

# 13. Interviewer Guidelines

## Do

* Ask open-ended questions.
* Ask about past behavior.
* Ask participants to describe specific situations.
* Ask follow-up questions.
* Listen more than you speak.
* Ask for examples.
* Allow silence.

Useful follow-up questions:

> Can you tell me more about that?

> What happened next?

> Why was that important?

> Can you give me an example?

> How did that make you feel?

> What did you do after that?

---

## Do Not

Do not ask leading questions.

Bad:

> Don't you think banking apps make it difficult to understand your spending?

Better:

> How easy or difficult is it for you to understand your spending?

---

Do not propose your solution.

Bad:

> Would an AI assistant help you understand your spending?

Better:

> What would make this process easier for you?

---

Do not try to convince participants.

Your role is to understand, not sell.

---

# 15. Research Reminder

The purpose of this interview is not to prove:

> "People need an AI Financial Copilot."

The purpose is to discover:

> "What problems do people actually experience when managing their finances?"

The research may eventually show that:

* an AI Copilot is useful;
* notifications are more useful;
* a better dashboard is enough;
* the original assumption is wrong.

All of these outcomes are valuable.
