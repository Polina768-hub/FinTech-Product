# Crypto User Discovery

## Product Discovery Exercise

### Goal

Apply the Product Discovery framework to understand how young adults interact with cryptocurrency and identify the most important challenges they experience.

The goal of this exercise is **not to create a crypto product immediately**.

The purpose is to:

1. Understand different types of young crypto users.
2. Explore their real behavior.
3. Understand how they enter and navigate the crypto ecosystem.
4. Identify recurring problems and pain points.
5. Understand how users currently solve these problems.
6. Validate which problems are frequent and important.
7. Identify product opportunities worth exploring further.

---

# 1. Problem Space

Cryptocurrency has become increasingly visible to young adults through social media, friends, online communities, investment platforms, and financial news.

However, being interested in cryptocurrency does not necessarily mean that users understand:

* how to start;
* which information to trust;
* which platform to use;
* how wallets work;
* what risks they are taking;
* how to make informed decisions;
* how to manage crypto assets after purchasing them.

This research aims to understand the real experiences of young adults when interacting with cryptocurrency.

---

## Initial Research Question

> **What challenges do young adults experience when exploring, entering, and participating in the cryptocurrency ecosystem?**

---

# 2. Research Objectives

This research aims to answer the following questions:

### User Behavior

* How do young adults first become interested in cryptocurrency?
* What triggers them to explore or buy cryptocurrency?
* How do they learn about crypto?
* Which platforms and tools do they use?

### Decision-Making

* How do users decide whether to buy cryptocurrency?
* What information influences their decisions?
* Who or what do they trust?
* How do they evaluate risk?

### Pain Points

* What is confusing for beginners?
* At which stage do users experience the most friction?
* What information is difficult to understand?
* What actions feel risky or stressful?

### Existing Alternatives

* How do users currently solve their problems?
* Where do they search for information?
* Which tools do they use?
* What do they like and dislike about existing solutions?

---

# 3. Target Users

The research will explore three initial user segments.

These segments are hypotheses and may change after research.

---

## Segment A — Crypto Curious

Young adults who:

* are interested in cryptocurrency;
* follow crypto-related content;
* have considered buying cryptocurrency;
* have never purchased crypto.

### Research Goal

Understand what prevents them from entering the crypto ecosystem.

Potential questions:

* What makes them interested?
* What stops them?
* What information do they need?
* What feels risky?

---

## Segment B — Crypto Beginners

Young adults who:

* have purchased cryptocurrency once or several times;
* have limited experience;
* are still learning how the ecosystem works.

### Research Goal

Understand the early user journey and identify points of confusion.

Potential questions:

* How did they make their first purchase?
* What was difficult?
* What did they not understand?
* What mistakes or concerns did they experience?

---

## Segment C — Active Crypto Users

Young adults who:

* regularly buy, sell, hold, or manage crypto assets;
* use one or more exchanges or wallets;
* have experience navigating the crypto ecosystem.

### Research Goal

Understand how experienced users manage information, decisions, and risks.

Potential questions:

* What tools do they use?
* What still frustrates them?
* What activities require the most effort?
* What problems remain unsolved?

---

# 4. Initial Assumptions

The following assumptions must be validated through research.

They are not facts.

---

## Assumption 1

Young adults interested in cryptocurrency struggle to identify trustworthy information.

### Importance

High.

### Uncertainty

High.

### Why it matters

If users cannot identify trustworthy information, they may make poor decisions or avoid entering the crypto ecosystem entirely.

### How I will test it

Ask:

> Where do you usually learn about cryptocurrency?

> How do you decide whether information is trustworthy?

> Tell me about the last time you encountered conflicting information about crypto.

---

## Assumption 2

Crypto beginners find the first purchase process confusing.

### Importance

High.

### Uncertainty

High.

### Why it matters

The onboarding experience may create a significant barrier to adoption.

### How I will test it

Ask:

> Tell me about the first time you decided to buy cryptocurrency.

> What happened step by step?

> What was confusing?

> What did you do when you did not understand something?

---

## Assumption 3

Young crypto users do not always understand the risks associated with their decisions.

### Importance

High.

### Uncertainty

High.

### Why it matters

Risk misunderstanding may lead to poor financial decisions and loss of trust.

### How I will test it

Ask:

> Before making a crypto-related decision, what risks did you consider?

> How did you evaluate those risks?

> What risks did you only learn about later?

---

## Assumption 4

Young adults feel overwhelmed by the amount of cryptocurrency-related information.

### Importance

Medium.

### Uncertainty

High.

### How I will test it

Ask:

> When you wanted to learn about crypto, where did you start?

> Was there anything confusing or overwhelming?

> How did you decide what information to ignore?

---

## Assumption 5

Social influence plays an important role in crypto-related decisions.

### Importance

Medium.

### Uncertainty

Medium.

### How I will test it

Ask:

> What influenced your first interest in cryptocurrency?

> Did friends, family, social media, or online communities influence your decisions?

---

## Assumption 6

Users struggle to understand why the value of their crypto portfolio changes.

### Importance

Medium.

### Uncertainty

High.

### How I will test it

Ask:

> Tell me about the last time your portfolio changed significantly.

> What did you do to understand why?

> Were you able to find a clear explanation?

# 5. Research Method

This research uses a mixed-method approach.

## Method 1 — Quantitative Survey

The survey will help identify:

* broad behavioral patterns;
* common challenges;
* popular information sources;
* confidence levels;
* differences between user segments.

### Target

30–50 responses.

### Survey Topics

* crypto experience;
* motivation;
* information sources;
* platforms and tools;
* challenges;
* confidence;
* risk understanding;
* current alternatives.

---

## Method 2 — Qualitative User Interviews

User interviews will help understand:

* the context behind user behavior;
* motivations;
* triggers;
* frustrations;
* decision-making processes;
* real examples of problems.

### Target

5–7 interviews.

### Interview Duration

Approximately 20–30 minutes.

```text

Research
↓

↓
Decision
```

# 17. Next Step — Solution Discovery

Do not choose a final product before completing Discovery.

After identifying a validated problem:

1. Select the strongest opportunity.
2. Generate multiple solution ideas.
3. Identify assumptions behind each solution.
4. Evaluate solution risks.
5. Prototype the strongest hypothesis.
6. Test the prototype with users.
7. Decide whether the solution should move toward an MVP.
